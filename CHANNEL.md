# History of Dread: Channel Bible

History of Dread is a YouTube and Instagram channel about what people feared, who feared it, and what actually happened. Brig directs and approves the work. Claude does most of it: research, scripts, source checks, the episode tracker, and the automation.

## The goal

The real goal is a monetised YouTube channel. Every decision gets weighed against that.

- **First milestone:** reach the YouTube Partner Program thresholds. As far as we know these are 1,000 subscribers plus either 4,000 public watch hours in 12 months or 10 million Shorts views in 90 days. Check the current numbers.
- **What helps:** weekly 10-minute episodes in Brig's own voice, daily Shorts, a mix of advertiser-friendly stories alongside the heavy ones, and strong titles and thumbnails.
- **What doesn't:** perfecting the automation before there's an audience.

## Episode format

Every episode has three parts:

1. **The dread:** what people feared, in their own words where possible.
2. **The person:** who expressed it, with enough background to make them real. One figure can stand for a wider fear shared by others at the time.
3. **The outcome:** what happened, and why.

## Outcome labels

- **Realised:** the fear came true.
- **Averted by action:** the threat was real, and people stopped it. Explain how.
- **Averted by luck:** it nearly happened, and no system stopped it.
- **Pure myth:** the threat was never real. Explain where the belief came from.
- **Ongoing:** unresolved.

A second label gives the category, such as war, plague, technology, the end of the world, or personal ruin.

## Scoring

- **Dread Score (shown at the start):** the average of three scores out of 10.
  - *Intensity:* how frightened people were.
  - *Reach:* how many people shared the fear.
  - *Stakes:* how bad it would have been if it came true.
- **Outcome Score (revealed at the end):** the same 0 to 10 scale, for how bad things actually turned out. 0 means no bad outcome at all.
- **Dread to Outcome:** the closing segment sets the two scores side by side and gives a verdict:
  - **Worse than feared** if the Outcome Score is higher.
  - **Better than feared** if it is lower.
  - **As feared** if they roughly match.
  - For Ongoing episodes, the Outcome Score is **Undecided**.

Example: the dread of World War II might score 7.3, but the outcome was a 10, so it was worse than feared.

## Deliverables per episode

- A long-form YouTube script
- A shot list
- A short vertical cut for Shorts and Instagram Reels
- An Instagram post with a portrait, a quote and a caption
- Sources, plus a list of things to verify before recording

## Length and schedule

- **Long-form:** about 10 minutes or more (roughly 1,500 words of narration and up). Anything over 8 minutes can carry mid-roll ads.
- **Cadence:** one long video a week, two at the most. Quality and fact-checking come before volume.
- **Shorts:** one a day, cut from the long episodes.

## Narration and footage

- Brig records the narration himself. No AI voice, to stay clear of YouTube's inauthentic-content rules.
- Prefer public-domain footage: US government material (National Archives, DVIDS, presidential libraries) and Prelinger civil-defence films.
- Don't lift network news clips (ABC, CBS, NBC) from YouTube. They trigger Content ID claims that take the ad money.
- Licensed archives (British Pathé, AP, Reuters) are expensive. Save them for when the channel is earning.
- Our own motion graphics and recreations count as original work and help with monetisation review.
- Heavy subjects like the Holocaust and nuclear war can get limited ads. Mix in lighter stories to keep revenue steady.

## Standards

- Never present a paraphrase or an interpretation as a direct quote. Mark anything unverified.
- Prefer primary sources: diaries, letters, interviews and newspapers from the period.
- Apply the same scrutiny to every claim, including the ones Brig brings in.
